<p>Written by: Jim Lambert</p>
<p>The Union Catholic girls delivered the most jaw-dropping collection of performances in meet history, and UC&#8217;s Jimmy Wischusen scored an emotional victory at Sunday&#8217;s 54th NJSIAA Meet of Champions at Ocean Breeze on Staten Island.</p>
<p>UC&#8217;s girls flexed their depth and talent to rack up a meet record five victories!!! Included in the unprecedented haul of gold medals, was a 1-2-3 sweep in the 800, and a meet record by <dfn><a href="https://ucvikings.com/sports/womens-indoor-track/roster/alexandra-bonn/1328" rel="smarttag" rev="1328">Alexandra Bonn</a></dfn> in the triple jump, and a relay sweep as the Vikings became the first team to ever win both the 4&#215;400 and 4&#215;800 at the same M of C.</p>
<p>Wischusen, who missed nearly the entire indoor season last year and most of the cross-country season this past fall with injuries, won the boys 3,200.</p>
<p>The final medal count for the Vikings was 15,11 for the girls and four for the boys.</p>
<p>Union Catholic&#8217;s epic day got rolling right away when the Vikings won the meet-opening 4&#215;800 relay in 9:28.77, nine second ahead of runner-up Westfield. But that doesn&#8217;t begin to tell the whole story.</p>
<p>UC won the 4&#215;800 without their three fastest half milers. They went with <a title="View athlete profile for Jimmiea King" href="https://nj.milesplit.com/athletes/12289897-jimmiea-king">Jimmiea King</a> (2:19.98), <a title="View athlete profile for Victoria Urbaez" href="https://nj.milesplit.com/athletes/11134431-victoria-urbaez">Victoria Urbaez</a> (2:22.42),<a title="View athlete profile for Alexandra Moore" href="https://nj.milesplit.com/athletes/11134427-alexandra-moore"> Alexandra Moore </a>(2:24.21), and<a title="View athlete profile for Leilani Gibson" href="https://nj.milesplit.com/athletes/10214180-leilani-gibson"> Leilani Gibson</a> (2:22.18). There&#8217;s that depth and talent pool that is unprecedented.</p>
<p>Next up for UC was<a title="View athlete profile for Alexandra Bonn" href="https://nj.milesplit.com/athletes/9695524-alexandra-bonn"> Alexandra Bonn</a> in the triple jump.</p>
<p>The senior not only successfully defended her title, but she soared to a meet and school record 39-6.25, which placed her No. 10 in state history. Bonn broke the meet record of 38-2, set by Pope John&#8217;s Teja Brown at the 2020 indoor Meet of Champions.</p>
<p>The Yale-bound Bonn, who also won the TJ at the M of C last spring, missed winning her fourth M of C title by an inch and half when she placed second in the long jump with an 18-0.<a title="View athlete profile for Lia Malave" href="https://nj.milesplit.com/athletes/9716835-lia-malave"> Lia Malave </a>of Toms River North went 18-1.25 on her final jump to win.</p>
<p><img decoding="async" src="https://assets.sp.milesplit.com/articles/329540/images/aska_55h_mocs_2023.png" alt="" /></p>
<p>Then it was <dfn><a href="https://ucvikings.com/sports/womens-indoor-track/roster/taylor-cox/1995" rel="smarttag" rev="1995">Taylor Cox&#8217;s</a></dfn> turn.</p>
<p>Cox, a sophomore at UC, scored one of the mot dramatic wins of the meet when she outleaned <a title="View athlete profile for Quaycian Davis" href="https://nj.milesplit.com/athletes/12652196-quaycian-davis">Quaycian Davis</a> of East Orange, 7.88 to 7.90. The time by Cox, who leaned so hard that she did a summersault after she crossed the line,  is No. 3 in meet history, tied for No. 5 in state history, and it&#8217;s No. 4 in the nation this season. The 7.90 by Davis is No. 4 in meet history, and No. 8 in NJ history.</p>
<p>On the track next for the Vikings were<a title="View athlete profile for Peyton Hollis" href="https://nj.milesplit.com/athletes/3891242-peyton-hollis"> Peyton Hollis</a>, <a title="View athlete profile for Kaleigh Gunsiorowski" href="https://nj.milesplit.com/athletes/10428253-kaleigh-gunsiorowski">Kaleigh Gunsiorowski</a>, and <a title="View athlete profile for Maameyaa Nyinah" href="https://nj.milesplit.com/athletes/9434141-maameyaa-nyinah">Maameyaa Nyinah</a>, who pulled of the 1-2-3 sweep, the second time a girls team has ever done that in any event in the 44 year history of the girls M of C. The North Hunterdon girls went 1-2-3 in the 3,200 in 1987.</p>
<p>Hollis, a junior, won in a school record 2:11.92 as she edged Gunsiorowski, second with a PR of 2:11.95, and Nyinah was third in 2:14.24, just ahead of<a title="View athlete profile for Kelsey Niglio" href="https://nj.milesplit.com/athletes/9437178-kelsey-niglio"> Kelsey Niglio</a> of Cherokee, fourth in 2:14.27. The victory for Hollis was especially satisfying because she has been building back up after missing the end of the cross-country season with an injury.</p>
<p>Nyinah, Cox, Gunsiorowski, and Hollis returned the track to run the 4&#215;400, and put the finishing touches on Union Catholic&#8217;s dominating day by finishing first in 3:52.16, the No. 1 time in the state this season,  No. 7 in meet history, and No. 5 in the country this season.</p>
<p>The 4&#215;400 splits for UC were 58.57 for the North Carolina-bound Nyinah, 57.54 for Cox, 58.03 for the Notre Dame-bound Gunsiorowski, and 58.03 for Hollis.</p>
<p>The three two medals for the girls came from sophomore sprinter Taylor Aska, second in the 55 dash in a school record 7.04, the second fastest in the state this season, and fifth in the 200 in 25.07, and from junior <dfn><a href="https://ucvikings.com/sports/womens-indoor-track/roster/leah-gould/1968" rel="smarttag" rev="1968">Leah Gould</a></dfn>, seventh in the 200 in 25.43.</p>
<p><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="https://ucvikings.com/images/2023/3/6/jw.jpeg" alt="track and field" width="620" height="458" /><br />
On the boys side, right after <a title="View athlete profile for Jimmy Wischusen" href="https://nj.milesplit.com/athletes/10214165-jimmy-wischusen">Jimmy Wischusen</a> came charging across the line to win the 3,200 in a Union County record 9:05.80, he was immediately overcome with emotion.</p>
<p>The junior at Union Catholic started thinking about everything he&#8217;s had to overcome to fulfill his dream of becoming a Meet of Champions winner.</p>
<p>&#8220;I&#8217;ve been through a lot of injuries and stuff throughout my high school career,&#8221; said Wischusen, who has shown a lot of heart and guts and resiliency to overcome all the adversity he&#8217;s dealt with. &#8220;This was my first track Meet of Champions I&#8217;ve ever run in, so to win this means a lot to me. I was very emotional after I won.&#8221;</p>
<p>Wischusen, who was battling a bad head cold on Sunday, didn&#8217;t mind the slower pace (4:40.39 for first 1,600), so he just ran relaxed until it was go time. With about 800 to go, he hit the gas and made a powerful surge that dropped the very talented field. Wischusen ran a 4:25.41 final 1,600 with a 2:09.50 final 800 and a 67.16 closing 400 to win by three-plus seconds over runner-up Nick Sullivan of CBA (9:09.01).</p>
<p>The other medalists for UC were sophomore Noah Cooper, second in the long jump with a 23-0.50, the 4&#215;400 relay, seventh in 3:24.36, and the 4&#215;800 relay, seventh in 8:05.33.</p>
