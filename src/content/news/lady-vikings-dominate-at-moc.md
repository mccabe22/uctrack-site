<h2 class="article-headline">UC&#8217;s Lufrano and 4&#215;400 relay team win titles at the NJSIAA T&amp;F Meet of Champions</h2>
<p class="article-subheadline">The Vkings have now an unprecedented five 4&#215;400 races at the prestigious meet</p>
<p class="article-byline">By Jim Lambert</p>
</header>
<p><dfn><a href="https://ucvikings.com/roster.aspx?rp_id=762" rel="smarttag" rev="762">Jerika Lufrano</a></dfn> ran the best race of her life to win the 1,600-meter run and the Union Catholic 4&#215;400-meter relay continued its stranglehold on the 4&#215;400-meter relay by blitzing the field to extend its remarkable streak to five titles in a row at the 41st NJSIAA Girls Track and Field  Meet of Champions on Sunday at the Bennett Center in Toms River.</p>
<p>The victory by Lufrano and the 4&#215;400 squad highlighted a sensational day for the Vikings as 15 girls competed,12 athletes placed in the top 10, and eight captured medals.</p>
<p>A couple weeks ago, Lufrano wasn&#8217;t even thinking about having a shot to win a Meet of Champions title</p>
<p>But that all changed last Tuesday, when Lufrano blasted a 4:54.68 anchor split for Union Catholic to bring the Vikings across the line first in the distance medley relay in a US#1 11:58.38 at the Eastern States Championships at the NY Armory.</p>
<p>That was the first time Lufrano ever broke 5 minutes.</p>
<p>&#8220;That race changed everything for me,&#8221; said Lufrano. &#8220;It gave me so much more confidence and made me feel like I did have a chance when I ran against the best in the state.&#8221;</p>
<p>Lufrano, a senior, took that confidence to the line with her at Sunday&#8217;s meet and executed her race plan to perfection when she won the 1,600 in a personal best, school record and NJ#5 4:59.26. Freshman Camryn Wennersten of Ridgewood was second in 4:59.73.</p>
<p>Lufrano, who broke her own school record of 5:02.92, threw her arms up in the air when she crossed the line and flashed a huge smile.<br />

&#8220;This means everything to me, my teammates, my friends, and my family,&#8221; said Lufrano, who also led off UC&#8217;s runner-up 4&#215;800 relay team (9:30.45) with a 2:21.4 carry. &#8220;I worked really hard for this my whole career and I&#8217;m just so happy right now.&#8221;</p>
<p>Lufrano and Wennersten broke away from the pack with three laps to go, and then Lufrano made a big push with 1,000 to go. Wennersten stayed right on Lufrano&#8217;s heels, but Lufrano hit the gas again with 150 meters left to open enough of a gap to push her to the victory.</p>
<p>Lufrano has come a long way in a year. Last year she was 16th in this race, and last spring she was seventh at the outdoor M of C in the 3,200.</p>
<p>What&#8217;s the difference between her from last year to this year?</p>
<p>&#8220;Just lots of hard work, lots of mileage, and lots of motivation,&#8221; said Lufrano. &#8220;And having confidence in yourself.&#8221;</p>

In the 4&#215;400, UC, which came into the race with a NJ#1 time of 3.50:93, won the race in 3:53.84 (No. 6 in meet history). Rancocas Valley was second in 3:57.82.</p>
<p>UC is the only team in meet history to win more than three 4&#215;400 titles in a row.</p>
<p>Senior <dfn><a href="https://ucvikings.com/roster.aspx?rp_id=712" rel="smarttag" rev="712">Khamil Evans</a></dfn>, out the last couple weeks with a sore hammy, put Union Catholic ahead to stay with a strong leadoff leg. Junior <dfn><a href="https://ucvikings.com/roster.aspx?rp_id=764" rel="smarttag" rev="764">Leena Morant</a></dfn> then blasted an indoor personal best of  57.3 to blow the race apart. Sophomore <dfn><a href="https://ucvikings.com/roster.aspx?rp_id=769" rel="smarttag" rev="769">Sydney Tucker</a></dfn> followed with a 58.9 as she maintained the huge lead, and then junior <dfn><a href="https://ucvikings.com/roster.aspx?rp_id=781" rel="smarttag" rev="781">Chi Chi Nkulume</a></dfn> capped it off with a 57.8 anchor carry.</p>
<p>Morant has now run on three indoor M of C winning 4&#215;400&#8217;s, and Evans two. This was the first time Tucker and Nkulume ran for UC on the 4&#215;400 at the M of C.</p>
<p>&#8220;I knew we had a big lead, so I just wanted to make sure no one could catch me,&#8221; said Nkulume. &#8220;I didn&#8217;t want let the girls on my team down. I knew they were counting on me. To be on this team and help us win our fifth in row is the best feeling in the world.&#8221;</p>
<p>Watch a video interview with the 4&#215;400 relay team<a href="https://nj.milesplit.com/videos/342566"> here</a></p>
<p>Tucker said the 4&#215;400 winning streak is something that team works hard tro continue.</p>
<p>&#8220;It&#8217;s definitely motivation,&#8221; Tucker said of the streak. &#8220;We always want to keep it going and we all ran strong and I&#8217;m proud of all of us for winning this again.&#8221;</p>
<p>Nkulume said it was a dream come true for her to win her first M of C title.</p>
<p>&#8220;It means everything for me to be out here at the Meet of Champions,&#8221; said Nkulume. &#8220;This is like a dream for me. I have never been part of something this special, so it feels great.&#8221;</p>
<p>Morant&#8217;s relay carry capped off a sensational meet for her.</p>
<p>Earlier in the meet, Morant placed second in a NJ#3/US#24 2:13.08, which is a school record, an indoor PR and No. 13 in meet history. Morant&#8217;s 2:13.08 broke the UC indoor record of 2:14.08 that was set in 2016 by Amari Onque-Shabazz.</p>
<p>&#8220;I had the school record on my mind  in the 800, so I really wanted to take that down and knew if I ran off of Victoria (Vanriele of Gov. Livingston, who won the race in 2:09.18), that the time would come with it. It was a very productive day, and I&#8217;m really proud of the 4&#215;400 for keeping our streak going..It was a great day for our whole team.&#8221;</p>
<p>In other UC highlights, <dfn><a href="https://ucvikings.com/roster.aspx?rp_id=769" rel="smarttag" rev="769">Sydney Tucker</a></dfn> placed third in the 55 dash in 7.16, Nkulume was fourth in the dash in 7.23 (7.22 in the trials), <dfn><a href="https://ucvikings.com/roster.aspx?rp_id=771" rel="smarttag" rev="771">Morgan Williams</a></dfn> placed 10th in the 800 in 2:18.43, <dfn><a href="https://ucvikings.com/roster.aspx?rp_id=717" rel="smarttag" rev="717">Hope Handlin</a></dfn> placed 11th in the pole vault by clearing 11-0, <dfn><a href="https://ucvikings.com/roster.aspx?rp_id=766" rel="smarttag" rev="766">Ashleigh Patterson</a></dfn> was 19th in the 2:22.46, freshman <dfn><a href="https://ucvikings.com/roster.aspx?rp_id=714" rel="smarttag" rev="714">Abby Granrath</a></dfn> placed 26tn in the 3,200 in 11:38.91, <dfn><a href="https://ucvikings.com/roster.aspx?rp_id=768" rel="smarttag" rev="768">Felicia Quainoo</a></dfn> ran the 10th fastest time in the hurdle trials (8.43), <dfn><a href="https://ucvikings.com/roster.aspx?rp_id=770" rel="smarttag" rev="770">Angelique Williams</a></dfn> finished 10th in the high jump a 5-2., and  <dfn><a href="https://ucvikings.com/roster.aspx?rp_id=779" rel="smarttag" rev="779">Jenna Keith</a></dfn> and <dfn><a href="https://ucvikings.com/roster.aspx?rp_id=759" rel="smarttag" rev="759">Morgan Jackson</a></dfn>ran on the runner-up 4&#215;800 relay.</p>
<p>&#8220;It was a great day,&#8221; said UC head coach <dfn><a href="https://ucvikings.com/staff.aspx?staff=2" rel="smarttag" rev="2">Mike McCabe</a></dfn>. &#8220;Jerika wins and breaks five, the 4&#215;400 ran great to win, Leena gets second with a school record, we got a third and fourth from Sydney and Chi Chi, and we got a bunch of medal winners. It was awesome. It couldn&#8217;t have gone much better.&#8221;</p>
<p>The Vikings, who are ranked No. 1 in the nation in the distance medley (11:59.38) and the 4&#215;800 (9:07.44), will be back in action when they chase some national relay titles at the New Balance Indoor Nationals (March 8-9-10) at the Armory in N.Y.</p>
